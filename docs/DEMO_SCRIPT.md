# Chorus demo script

Target length: 90 seconds. Record at 1920x1080 or 1440x900 with browser zoom at 100%. Open `/demo`, keep the cursor outside the graph, and click **Restart** immediately before recording.

The walkthrough advances automatically, then pauses at both human gates. The timings below leave room for natural delivery. Do not rush to fill every quiet second. Let the graph movement carry part of the explanation.

## Talk track

### 0:00 to 0:08 - Set up the problem

> A podcast contains dozens of possible posts and clips, but most repurposing tools treat all of them as equally worth making. Chorus starts with one episode and one growth objective, then uses seven agents to build one coherent campaign.

Keep the **Agent flow** tab open. The graph will move through ingest and transcription.

### 0:08 to 0:18 - Show source judgment

> First, Chorus verifies the source, creates word-level timestamps, and maps the episode into moments that can stand on their own. The Source Analyst found five candidates here, but only four were strong enough to use.

Let the camera settle on **analyze**, then **strategize**.

### 0:18 to 0:30 - Explain planning and the first human gate

> The Strategist gives every output a different job, and the Content Director challenges that plan before anything expensive is produced. This is the first human checkpoint. I can approve the strategy or send specific changes back into the graph.

When the strategy gate appears, click **Evidence**. Point out the selected topics and the deliberately rejected topic. Say:

> The rejected column matters. Chorus is not trying to maximize the number of outputs. It is showing what it chose not to make, and why.

Return to **Agent flow** and click **Approve strategy**.

### 0:30 to 0:49 - Show one-asset-at-a-time production

> Production happens one asset at a time. The Writing Agent grounds every claim in an exact transcript quote, and the Clip Producer selects boundaries, crops the source, and renders word captions. Each draft goes straight to an independent Critic.

Keep the cursor still while the graph moves between **produce** and **critique**.

### 0:49 to 1:02 - Land the revision loop

> This first thread is accurate, but the opening is generic. The Critic returns only the blocking feedback, so Chorus revises that asset instead of restarting the campaign. The second pass clears the bar at 8.8.

Pause briefly when the coral revision edge remains visible and the visit count increments.

### 1:02 to 1:15 - Land the portfolio replan

> Passing assets individually is not enough. The Campaign Reviewer sees the whole set and catches two outputs making the same eval argument. Diversity falls to 54, which forces a replan in code. Chorus keeps the three good assets, replaces only the repetitive clip, and reviews the portfolio again.

Let the graph travel through **campaign review**, **replan**, **produce**, and back to **campaign review**.

> The revised portfolio reaches 86 for diversity and moves to the final human gate.

### 1:15 to 1:30 - Finish on the deliverable

Click **Approve campaign**. The interface will switch to **Outputs** when packaging completes.

> The result is not a folder of disconnected drafts. It is a reviewed campaign: vertical clips, platform-native writing, source timestamps, Critic scorecards, and an exportable package. The demo is deterministic, but it mirrors the same resumable graph the worker runs on a real podcast.

Select the first short video and hold on its captioned preview for three to four seconds. End the recording there.

## Delivery notes

- Say “judgment system” once. Avoid calling Chorus a pipeline.
- Let both human gates sit on screen for at least two seconds before clicking.
- Do not open the full event timeline during the recording. It is evidence for questions, not part of the main story.
- If you need a 60-second cut, skip the Evidence tab and summarize the rejected topic in one sentence.
- If asked what is mocked, answer directly: the walkthrough data and timing are local and deterministic. The graph, routing rules, revision budgets, gates, and production architecture mirror the real worker.

## One-sentence close

> Chorus does not ask, “How much content can we extract?” It asks, “What is worth making, and does this campaign work as a whole?”
